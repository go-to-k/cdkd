- **A force-quit or a closed terminal mid-deploy now leaves a rollback journal, so `cdkd rollback` can revert it (issue [#6894](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/6894))** -- `src/deployment/force-quit-journal.ts`, `src/deployment/deploy-engine/execute.ts` (+ `execute/force-quit-flush.ts`), `src/cli/commands/deploy/{helpers,command}.ts`, `src/cli/pipe-close-handler.ts`. A second Ctrl-C (or the `SIGTERM` a cancelled CI job sends after its `SIGINT`) exited without writing the journal, which a deploy wrote only once its operations settled, and a `SIGHUP` killed the process at once with no message; the resources already changed stayed changed and `cdkd rollback` answered `Nothing to roll back`. The force-quit now writes one `interrupted` segment, inside its existing 5-second exit budget and beside the asset-upload abort: the operations completed so far, plus those still in flight as failed operations (`--revert-failed` governs them), through the same secret redaction as every journal write. It then prints the `cdkd rollback` command and exits `130`. A `SIGHUP` force-quits the same way, stops new operations, and exits `129`; an `EIO` on a hung-up terminal's output no longer kills the process first. An uninterrupted deploy makes no extra call, and a single Ctrl-C behaves as before. A `SIGKILL` or power loss is still uncovered (`docs/cli-rollback-limitations.md`).
