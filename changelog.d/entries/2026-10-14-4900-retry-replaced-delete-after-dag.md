- **A replacement whose old resource is still in use by a resource the same deploy has not updated yet now deletes it after the rest of the deploy, instead of leaving it behind (issue [#4900](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/4900))** -- `src/deployment/deploy-engine/replacement.ts` (`deleteReplacedAfterCreate` defers its first failure; new `retryDeferredReplacedDeletes` / `warnUnretriedReplacedDeletes`), `src/deployment/deploy-engine/execute.ts`, `src/deployment/deploy-engine.ts`, and a new unit file. A create-first replacement deleted the old resource right after creating the new one, before the dependents had moved to it. When a dependent still used the old resource, AWS refused the delete. For example, an `AWS::Lambda::WebFunctionEndpoint` refuses the delete of its `AWS::Lambda::WebFunctionRevision` with a 409 `ConflictException`. cdkd warned `Failed to delete old resource` and the old resource stayed in AWS, untracked, on every replacing deploy. Now the first failure inside a deploy is retried once after its creates, updates and deletes have all completed, as CloudFormation deletes old resources in its cleanup phase. The fix is not specific to any resource type. A retry that fails warns as before, and a retry the provider skips makes the row a partial update. A deploy that fails or is interrupted first does not retry, and it warns `Failed to delete old resource ... (not retried: ...)`. The retry runs under the row's own masking, stack-records and delete-guard scopes, and records its skip and guard events as the row's. A delete that succeeds inline is unchanged, so an ordinary deploy makes no extra call.
