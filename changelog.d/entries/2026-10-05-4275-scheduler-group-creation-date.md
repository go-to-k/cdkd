- **A Scheduler Schedule created or imported from this version on, whose `GroupName` comes from a secret, can be updated in place and destroyed; a rotated secret can no longer make `--replace` or a rollback write a schedule or DB proxy target group into another environment (issue [#4275](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/4275))** -- `src/provisioning/providers/scheduler-schedule-provider.ts`, `src/provisioning/providers/rds-dbproxy-targetgroup-provider.ts`. State keeps a secret-derived group as its `{{resolve:...}}` reference and the schedule's ARN embeds the group, so every update of such a schedule was refused and its destroy skipped it. cdkd now records each schedule's AWS creation date (attribute `cdkd:CreationDate`) and checks it, with the recorded target ARN and role, against the schedule the group the secret resolves to holds: an update or rollback revert goes ahead only on a match, and a destroy finds the schedule by them in any group. A secret rotated onto another schedule, or onto no schedule under the same reference, is refused without the `--replace` fallback; a re-pointed reference stays a group change. A record from before this version with a secret-derived group stays refused and skipped: delete the schedule by hand, drop its record with `cdkd orphan`, and deploy again. A DBProxyTargetGroup whose proxy name now addresses another target group (or none), on a deploy or a rollback revert, is refused the same way: under `UpdateReplacePolicy: Retain` the replacement only created, registering the targets on the proxy the rotated secret named. Tests: `tests/unit/provisioning/{scheduler-schedule-provider,secret-reference-immutable-sites}.test.ts`, `tests/unit/deployment/deploy-engine-replace.test.ts`; integ arm in `tests/integration/secret-derived-immutable-names/`.
