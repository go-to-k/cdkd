- **A `cdkd refactor` interrupted mid-move is now safe and finishes itself on the next run (issue [#5212](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/5212))** -- `src/cli/commands/refactor-move.ts`, `src/cli/commands/refactor.ts`, `src/state/pending-moves.ts` (new), `src/utils/deferred-interrupts.ts` (new), `src/types/state.ts` (`pendingMovesOut`), `src/deployment/deploy-engine/deploy-flow.ts`, `src/cli/commands/destroy-runner.ts`, `src/cli/commands/diff-recursive.ts`, `docs/cli-refactor.md`, new unit files, and new phases in the `refactor-move` integ. Before, a refactor killed between the two state writes of a move left the resource in no cdkd state, and the destination's next deploy could create a second one; Ctrl+C also skipped every `finally`, stranding the stack locks. Now SIGINT / SIGTERM is held until the writes finish, and a move first records itself in the source stack's record (`pendingMovesOut`, ids only) while keeping the resource, then adds it to the destination, then drops it from the source. While the source records an unfinished move, `cdkd deploy` and `cdkd destroy` of it refuse and `cdkd diff` exits 3, and the next `cdkd refactor` of it finishes the move. The check is one field of the record a deploy already reads: no extra S3 or AWS call. The field is additive, so no schema bump: an older binary drops it on its next save and does not refuse.
