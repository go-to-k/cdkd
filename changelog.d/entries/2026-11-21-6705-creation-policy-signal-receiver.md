- **`CreationPolicy` signals are waited on and `cfn-init` finds its metadata, through a CloudFormation signal receiver stack (issue [#6705](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/6705))** -- `src/deployment/deploy-engine/signal-receiver.ts` (new), `src/deployment/deploy-engine/signal-receiver-run.ts` (new), `src/deployment/deploy-engine/deploy-flow.ts`, `src/deployment/deploy-engine/execute/provision-node.ts`, `src/deployment/intrinsic-resolver/functions/pseudo-parameter.ts`, `src/types/state/stack-state.ts`, `src/cli/commands/destroy-runner/under-lock.ts`, `src/cli/commands/export/changeset.ts`, `src/cli/commands/state/orphan.ts`. `cfn-signal --resource` and `cfn-init` call the CloudFormation API, which a cdkd deploy had no stack to answer, and CDK grants the instance role those calls on `AWS::StackId` only. A stack declaring a `CreationPolicy` `ResourceSignal` or `AWS::CloudFormation::Init` metadata now gets a CloudFormation stack with its exact name, kept for the stack's lifetime and recorded in state (`signalReceiverStackId`, no schema bump); `AWS::StackId` resolves to its real ARN, so CDK's grant matches. Each deploy that creates or replaces a signalling resource arms a wait condition for it (signal count and `Metadata`), and the resource completes only on its signals (a `FAILURE` or the `Timeout` fails the deploy and the rollback deletes it). `cdkd destroy` deletes the receiver, `cdkd export` deletes it before taking the name, `cdkd state orphan` keeps it and prints the delete command. A stack declaring neither makes no CloudFormation call; a same-name CloudFormation stack cdkd did not make, a denied call, a nested stack or `NoEcho` metadata keep the warn-and-proceed behavior. A wait condition without a `Handle` is no longer refused.
