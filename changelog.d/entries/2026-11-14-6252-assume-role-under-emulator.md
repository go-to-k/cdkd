- **`--assume-role` under `--aws-emulator` now runs the workload as the role, and mixed mode no longer assumes an emulator role in real AWS (issue [#6252](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/6252))** -- `src/local/local-aws-wiring.ts`, `src/local/role-permission-emulation.ts`, `src/cli/commands/local-invoke/{credentials,execution-role}.ts`, `local-start-api/{credentials,assume-role}.ts`, `local-invoke-agentcore/env.ts`, `local-run-task/image-context.ts`, unit tests, `docs/local-emulation.md`, `docs/aws-emulator.md`. Before, `local invoke --assume-role`, `local start-api --assume-role-auto` and `local invoke-agentcore --assume-role` assumed the role in the emulator and then replaced its credentials with the emulator's own keys, so the handler ran unrestricted while cdkd said it ran as the role. The role's credentials the emulator issued now take the emulator keys' place, as `run-task --assume-task-role`'s already did, and the emulator enforces the role's policies. When the role's trust policy refuses the emulator's user, cdkd no longer falls back to `sts:GetFederationToken` against the emulator (that session was allowed nothing, so every call was denied); it warns that the trust policy must allow the emulator's identity. In mixed mode, a bare role flag reads the role from the state record only when `Parameters` excepts IAM, as `run-task` does; otherwise it warns with the `AWS_ENDPOINT_URL_IAM` / explicit-ARN remedy instead of sending an account-000000000000 ARN to real STS, and `local invoke` drops its "Re-run with --assume-role" hint.
