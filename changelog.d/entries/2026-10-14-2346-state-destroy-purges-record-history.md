- **`cdkd state destroy` now removes the earlier versions of the state records it deletes, so their bodies stop being readable by version id (issue [#2346](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/2346) sites 1-3)** -- `src/state/s3-state-backend.ts` (`purgeDeletedStateHistory`), `src/cli/commands/destroy-runner.ts` (`purgeStateHistory`), `src/cli/commands/state.ts`, `src/provisioning/nested-stack-context.ts` and `src/provisioning/providers/nested-stack-provider.ts`, new `tests/unit/state/state-destroy-version-purge.test.ts`. The state bucket is versioned, so the record delete used to write only a delete marker, and every earlier `state.json` body (every recorded property and attribute) stayed readable through `GetObject` with a `VersionId`. After the delete and the kept-resource record's write, the command now purges the region-scoped key's noncurrent versions, plus those of a cascading nested child. It also purges the legacy `<prefix>/<stack>/state.json` key's history, but only when no other region of the stack has `state.json` versions, current or earlier, because that history may belong to that region. Otherwise, or when the record's absence could not be confirmed or the other regions could not be listed, the history is kept and a warning says so. **Every other delete keeps the history as the recovery path**: `cdkd destroy`, `cdkd state orphan`, rollback, export and the v1 -> v2 migration tail. That split is the maintainer's decision on the issue. The purge needs `s3:ListBucketVersions` and `s3:DeleteObjectVersion`. Without them the destroy still succeeds, and a warning names both grants.
