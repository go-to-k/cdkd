- **`cdkd scrub` / `cdkd deploy` no longer print a recorded secret that an invisible character had split, and `scrub --fail` now reports such a key instead of passing over it** (issue [#2874](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/2874)). Four warning sites in `src/deployment/outputs-export-alias.ts` decided "does this name hold a secret" from the RAW name and then printed a SANITISED one. `stripControlChars` DELETES its class, so a plaintext split by one of those characters is absent from the raw name and contiguous in the printed text: the verdict said safe and the message published the secret at `warn`, into the CI logs the `--dry-run --fail` gate runs in. Measured, eight of ten tested characters reconstituted the plaintext verbatim; the two that did not printed it one character short.

  Every printed name now goes through `secretSafeKeyDisplay`, whose verdict, masking and returned text all happen in one canonical string space, derived from Unicode categories rather than enumerated. The mechanism and the design that was measured still leaking are on that function's doc comment.

  **Two user-visible consequences.** `cdkd scrub --fail` exits `1` on a state key it previously passed over — that key was already leaking; the change is what cdkd can see, not what the state holds. Rotate the secret and change the `Export.Name`. And a warning may print `<name withheld: contains a secret>` where masking cannot leave a safe name; the collision and its remedy are still named.

  Changed: `src/deployment/outputs-export-alias.ts`, `src/cli/commands/scrub.ts`, `src/deployment/deploy-engine.ts`, `src/utils/display-safe.ts` (a back-pointer only). Fences: `tests/unit/deployment/secret-safe-key-display.test.ts` and `secret-scan-class-superset.test.ts`, which scans the BMP to prove the class covers both sanitisers'. The remaining `stripControlChars`-alone sites keep the narrower class — issue [#2888](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/2888).
