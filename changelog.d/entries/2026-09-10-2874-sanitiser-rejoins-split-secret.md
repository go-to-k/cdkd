- **`cdkd scrub` / `cdkd deploy` no longer print a recorded secret that an invisible character had split, and `scrub --fail` now reports such a key instead of passing over it** (issue [#2874](https://github.com/go-to-k/cdkd/issues/2874)). Three warning sites in `src/deployment/outputs-export-alias.ts` decided "does this name hold a secret" from the RAW name and then printed a SANITISED one. `stripControlChars` DELETES its class, so a plaintext split by one of those characters is absent from the raw name and contiguous in the printed text: the verdict said safe and the message published the secret at `warn`, into the CI logs the `--dry-run --fail` gate runs in. Measured against the shipped code, eight of ten tested characters reconstituted the plaintext verbatim; the two that did not printed it one character short. `exportAliasCollisionScrubWarning` was the worst of the three because it prints even when the verdict misses, so it needed only ONE recorded secret rather than two.

  Every printed name now goes through `secretSafeKeyDisplay`, whose verdict, masking and returned text all happen in one canonical string space, widened beyond both sanitisers to the zero-width formatters. The mechanism, the design that was measured still leaking, and why the caller's own exposure is threaded as force-mask needles are on that function's doc comment.

  **Two user-visible consequences.** `cdkd scrub --fail` exits `1` on a state key it previously passed over — that key was already leaking; the change is what cdkd can see, not what the state holds. Rotate the secret and change the `Export.Name`. And a warning may print `<name withheld: contains a secret>` where masking cannot leave a safe name; the collision and its remedy are still named.

  Changed: `src/deployment/outputs-export-alias.ts`, `src/cli/commands/scrub.ts`, `src/deployment/deploy-engine.ts`. Fence: `tests/unit/deployment/secret-safe-key-display.test.ts`.
